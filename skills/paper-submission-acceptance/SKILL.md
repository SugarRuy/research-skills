# Paper Submission Acceptance

## Purpose and scope

Assess whether the manuscript is ready for submission: an independent reader should understand why the question matters, why the design answers it, what new knowledge the evidence supports, and where the conclusions apply.

Use the criteria below as an editorial readiness rubric, not a prediction of acceptance. Adapt evidence expectations to the research type: experiments, proofs, qualitative analysis, and synthesis support different kinds of claims. This skill is self-contained and requires no specific project structure, tools, or companion skills.

Use it to decide whether a paper needs substantive work, can enter finishing, or is ready to submit. An assessment alone does not request manuscript edits or submission. When fixes are also requested, carry out the authorized work without imposing a new item-by-item approval process.

## Acceptance criteria

### 1. A worthwhile question and a clear contribution

- Identify the question, its importance, the specific gap in prior work, and what the community learns from this paper.
- A research question should expose an interesting unresolved issue, not merely paraphrase the experiment inventory.
- Explain how a framework, method, dataset, or evaluation enables the substantive contribution. A new name does not establish novelty; an empirical paper need not manufacture a new algorithm.
- Check that the abstract, introduction, and conclusion express a consistent contribution with evidence-matched strength.

### 2. A connected argument from question to conclusion

- Each major experiment must have a purpose, an intelligible comparison, and a role in answering the question.
- Methods explain why the important design choices are needed before overwhelming the reader with operations or notation.
- Results progress through questions, observations, and implications. A sequence of tables or metrics is not sufficient organization.
- Section openings should orient the reader at the appropriate level. Do not demand repetitive roadmaps, a fixed section order, or stronger claims at every paragraph opening.

### 3. Evidence sufficient for the actual claims

- Assess controls, baselines, measurement validity, coverage, and alternative explanations against the claims the paper actually makes.
- Distinguish observational associations, intervention effects, exploratory analyses, and demonstrated mechanisms. Similar point estimates do not establish equivalence; a proxy improvement does not automatically establish the desired outcome.
- Treat missing controls or experiments as blockers only when their absence materially undermines a central claim. Explain what uncertainty the proposed addition would resolve.
- Do not demand universally positive results, wins over every baseline, arbitrary numbers of evaluated systems or datasets, or an exhaustive experiment matrix.
- Where appropriate, consider narrowing a claim using existing evidence before demanding new experiments. Preserve adverse results and genuine uncertainty.

### 4. Main-text evidence is sufficient to assess the main argument

Substantive numerical results used in the main text should be directly displayed in a main table or figure, or be transparently derivable from displayed values under the stated estimand. This includes numbers used to support an interpretation, comparison, or conclusion, even when they are not the headline result. For other research types, provide the corresponding proof structure, qualitative evidence, or synthesis basis needed to assess the main argument.

- A value existing in an internal results file establishes provenance, not reader-facing support. An appendix-only citation does not satisfy this main-text display requirement.
- For a core result, provide a suitable main-text display. For a secondary result, move the detailed numerical claim and its dependent interpretation to the appendix, retaining only the supported main-text summary. Whether a number is substantive depends on its role in the argument, not on where it currently appears.
- Do not expand every table simply to preserve incidental numbers. Select evidence by its contribution to the argument.
- Distinguish result estimates from experimental design counts, mathematical constants, and explicitly chosen illustrative thresholds; do not mechanically force those into result tables.
- Check denominators, eligible populations, pairing, aggregation, and rounding before accepting a claimed derivation. Do not substitute pooled estimates for unweighted group averages, subtract incompatible conditional rates, or reconstruct paired effects from rounded marginal summaries.
- Appendices may contain intervals, detailed tests, disaggregated results, subsets, and robustness analyses. Keep the main text's material uncertainty and scope understandable without moving the entire argument into the appendix.

### 5. Figures, tables, and terminology reduce reading effort

- Align the names used in method definitions, table rows or figure labels, and result discussion. Natural abbreviated references are fine once the referent is clear.
- Explain concrete operations and comparison objects. A citation or elaborate label does not replace a definition.
- A reader should be able to identify conditions, baseline, outcome, units, direction, and any denominator distinction needed for interpretation.
- Captions should communicate the table's purpose or key supported observation and necessary reading instructions. Avoid duplicating the entire method or assuming every caption needs a rigid template.
- Retain useful short labels; do not replace them with cumbersome descriptions merely to eliminate abbreviations.
- Judge the rendered figure or table, including its surrounding page. Numerical correctness alone does not establish readability.

### 6. Information has a clear hierarchy and claims remain proportionate

- Keep the core question, design rationale, essential definitions, and decisive evidence in the main text.
- Put complete protocols, instruments, detailed scoring, statistical procedures, and supplementary findings in a navigable appendix when needed. Keep operational logs outside the paper unless they affect scientific interpretation or reproducibility.
- Establish content and logic before compression. At final submission, satisfy the applicable length limit without silently deleting essential evidence or distorting the template.
- Prefer direct prose that adds information. Do not turn a locally approved sentence style into a mandate for stronger claims throughout the paper.
- Limitations should be concise, specific, and defensible. Preserve real weaknesses and material scope boundaries without presenting every unattempted extension as a fatal flaw or repeating caveats throughout the paper.

### 7. The final delivery is actually verified

- Confirm consistency among the current manuscript, tables, figures, appendix, analysis outputs, and generators where available. Identify the version inspected.
- Separate bibliographic accuracy from whether a source supports the adjacent claim. Verify publication status and attribution when checking references.
- Inspect the actual final PDF for legibility, layout defects, broken references, and navigation. Do not infer visual quality from extracted text.
- Resolve page waste by understanding its cause; do not fill space with redundant prose, stretch tables, or treat every short line as a defect.
- For a final submission decision, verify applicable official submission requirements, anonymity, and required package contents. If the submission requirements or materials are unavailable, leave this dimension unverified while completing the substantive assessment.
- Retain a traceable manuscript and evidence version. A successful local check or earlier build cannot certify the whole current submission.

## How to assess

Read the current manuscript and relevant figures/tables first as a reader. Use available trackers and author decisions to recover intended scope and established evidence, not as substitutes for the manuscript. Later decisions supersede earlier proposals; assistant suggestions are not author requirements.

Build a compact internal map of central questions, claims, comparisons, and evidence locations. Inspect relevant appendix material before declaring evidence missing. Reuse applicable checks from the same version; recheck affected surfaces when content or sources change.

Search external sources when evaluating novelty, citation accuracy, or current submission requirements requires verification. Do not upload private manuscript text for searches. A local readiness assessment does not require an unrelated literature sweep.

If independent review would resolve a material reader-comprehension uncertainty, use a fresh reader with the manuscript and a neutral task. Ask them to explain the question, experiment purposes, main table readings, and supported contribution. Do not require a panel on every pass or present simulated perspectives as independent reviews.

If a specific unresolved check needs specialized review, citation verification, or package validation, use an available tool or companion skill only when relevant. Do not require a particular skill family, automatically launch every check, or inherit unrelated report templates. Keep one readiness judgment.

## Decision and report

Lead with one supported status:

- **Substantive revision needed:** a demonstrated gap in contribution, design, evidence, or argument prevents the central claims from being assessed or sustained.
- **Ready for finishing:** the central research and argument are stable; bounded presentation, appendix, reference, or package work remains.
- **Ready to submit:** the substantive criteria and applicable final checks pass for the inspected version.
- **Readiness not fully verified:** missing materials or checks prevent a complete verdict. Still state what can be concluded about substantive readiness.

Report the inspected version and coverage, then the decision-relevant findings. For each issue, give a location, the evidence, its effect on readiness, and a concrete completion condition. Separate:

- **Substantive blockers**
- **Required finishing work**
- **Unverified checks**
- **Optional improvements**, only when useful

Do not equate unavailable evidence with a demonstrated failure. Do not average a decisive blocker away with strong scores elsewhere. Default to a concise assessment rather than numerical scoring, an acceptance probability, or a long referee report.

State what already meets the standard, and what must happen before the verdict changes. If reporting in an existing tracker is requested, update it rather than creating parallel ledgers.

## Stopping rule

Stop substantive revision when the contribution and question are stable, central claims have sufficient accessible evidence, and readers face no material obstacle to understanding the argument. Remaining local wording or layout preferences need not reopen the research.

For repeated passes, preserve resolved decisions and stable criteria. Reopen an issue only for new evidence, a relevant manuscript change, or an actual contradiction. Do not demand the uniquely best wording, invent new acceptance criteria, or keep proposing improvements after the requested acceptance work is complete.
