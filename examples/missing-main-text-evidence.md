# Example: numerical evidence appears only in the appendix

This example is fictional. It illustrates a failure of the main-text evidence requirement and two ways to resolve it. It is not an assessment of a real manuscript or a benchmark result.

## Supplied materials

A manuscript, appendix, analysis outputs, and rendered PDF are available. Assume the other applicable readiness checks pass.

Section 4 states: “In condition B, the error rate falls from 20% to 14%, a 30% relative reduction.” The next paragraph uses that result to argue that the improvement also holds under condition B. The main tables show only aggregate results. Appendix Table A3 contains the condition-specific rates for the same eligible population and aggregation, and the analysis outputs agree with those rates.

Assume the broader contribution is supported by the main results. The condition-specific finding is supplementary to that contribution, but the numbers still perform substantive work in the main-text argument.

## Verdict: Ready for finishing

The result has been checked against the supplied evidence, but its placement does not meet the skill's main-text display requirement. A bounded change to evidence placement or discussion is required before the manuscript is ready to submit. No new experiment is needed on the facts supplied here.

## Required finishing work

**Location:** Section 4, the condition B numerical claim and its dependent interpretation.

**Evidence:** The reported reduction is consistent with Appendix Table A3: `(20 - 14) / 20 = 30%`. However, readers cannot inspect or derive it from the main tables. A correct analysis output and an appendix citation establish provenance, but do not satisfy the main-text display requirement.

**Effect on readiness:** The main text uses a numerical result that it does not display. Calling the result “secondary” does not exempt the retained numerical claim from the requirement. The issue blocks a Ready to submit verdict, while remaining finishing work under the stated assumptions.

**Completion condition — choose the route that fits the argument:**

- **Retain the detailed claim in the main text:** Add a compact main-text display of the condition-specific rates, with the condition, population, metric, and aggregation clear. Reference that display from the discussion.
- **Keep the detailed result in the appendix:** Move both the numerical claim and its dependent condition-specific interpretation to the appendix. A main-text pointer such as “Additional condition-specific results are reported in Appendix A” may remain. Preserve only conclusions independently supported by the main-text evidence.

Deleting the numbers while retaining the same unsupported condition-specific interpretation does not resolve the issue.

## When the classification would change

The finishing verdict relies on the supplied evidence supporting the finding and the central argument standing without it. If this result is indispensable to the central contribution, retain enough main-text evidence to assess that contribution; moving it away cannot repair the main argument. If inspection instead reveals incompatible populations or an unsupported effect, assess that as an evidence or claim problem, not merely a display problem.

## Next pass

Check the chosen change in the current manuscript and rendered PDF, including any affected references or page constraints. Reopen the numerical analysis only if its inputs or interpretation change. Once this issue is resolved and the other applicable checks still pass, change the verdict to Ready to submit.
